What changed in the rebuild,
and why it matters

Both models ask the same question: how much is a player's future production worth, after paying his contract? The rebuild changes how that production is forecast, how historical decisions are reconstructed, and how uncertainty enters the valuation.

The previous model already includes aging, survival, and RFA rights. The rebuild replaces or revises those calculations. It does not simply add them to a model that ignored them.

The problems and the responses

What the rebuild addresses

Some changes repair errors in historical valuation. Others replace forecasting choices or make the economic assumptions explicit. Those are different reasons to prefer a model.

IssueWhy it matters in the previous modelWhat the rebuild changes
Information from the futureA historical forecast can use future seasons indirectly because the aging curve was fitted on the full panel. A contract's start year also need not be the year it was signed. A 2017 extension starting in 2019 must be valued using what was known in 2017.Forecast fits use earlier seasons; price fits use earlier signings. Contract status is read at the decision date. Tests change future data and check that earlier answers stay fixed.
The skater contract-status input still assumes the vendor snapshot is complete for contracts ending from 2018.
Recent results are an imperfect ability estimateA season total combines skill, games played, and temporary good or bad performance. Multiplying that starting total through an aging curve can carry its noise into several future seasons.Estimate performance and playing time separately, reduce reliance on noisy recent results, and add annual aging changes. The aging fit also adjusts for the fact that players who disappear leave no next-season performance to observe.
The adjustment does not eliminate selection problems. The rebuild still forecasts too much decline for stars.
A mean hides the downside and upsideA single expected path cannot tell you how likely a bad contract outcome is. It also cannot fully represent rules such as a minimum price or the option to decline an RFA qualifying offer.Simulate careers, allowing errors to persist across seasons, and value the paths separately. In the rights calculation, a club can stop qualifying a player using the history it has observed.
The simulation draws uncertainty in total season production, rather than separate skill and games shocks. Persistence and return assumptions are approximations.
RFA status can reveal a later decisionProduction treats an expiry label such as “UFA no QO” as zero retained rights. That can use the club's later decision to decline an offer to erase a right the club held at signing.Establish the control window from eligibility, then model the decision to retain or release the player. Future offer rules are dated too.
The stopping policy approximates the best decision rule, and average salary can misstate the final-year qualifying offer.
The dollar scale needs an explicit meaningA price estimated from observed performance is not automatically the price of an uncertain forecast. Valuations also depend on whether securing several years is itself something the club is buying.Fit prices to signing-date forecasts. The primary currency includes contract length; a version excluding length remains a sensitivity. Forecast and realised production are scored on the same price relationship.
This changes the economic comparison. It does not establish a universal dollar value for a win.

What “surplus” means here. A positive rebuilt value means the contract costs less than the estimated market price of a comparable production forecast. Because contracts supply the price scale, this is a comparison with market behaviour. It is not proof of team profit or proof that the market mispriced the player.

On these samples, the rebuild reduces average season error by about 10% and average dollar error by about 5%. The season improvement is consistent when players are repeatedly resampled. The dollar advantage is weaker, especially on a score that penalises large misses more heavily.

The dollar test puts both forecasts and the realised outcomes through the same rebuilt price relationship. It tests forecast usefulness in one currency, rather than comparing two full valuation chains against different dollar targets.

Reasons to prefer the candidate

  • It performs better on the matched skater forecast sample.
  • Historical inputs and model fits are tied more carefully to the decision date.
  • It supplies a distribution of contract outcomes, which the thesis can use to describe risk.

Reasons to keep qualifications

  • Stars remain under-forecast at longer horizons.
  • Uncertainty ranges pass tests across all contracts together, but can still miss too often for particular groups.
  • The later 2022-2025 pages were inspected during development; they are not an untouched final test.

Goalies require a narrower claim. Their rebuilt branch retains production's ability projector, uses participation without contract inputs, and allows a different goalie price level. It is a frozen sensitivity with recorded limitations; the skater improvement above does not establish a goalie improvement.

Large valuation changes need care. The two full chains disagree sharply on long contracts. That combines forecasting, pricing, and valuation-date differences. The comparison has not established how much each causes, or which full-chain value is correct.

The decision. The evidence supports carrying the rebuilt candidate into the later cross-asset comparison, while retaining the previous model and the rebuild without contract-status inputs as sensitivities. The Player Model candidate is complete with limitations recorded. Adopting it for the thesis remains a separate decision when Picks and Prospects work resumes.
