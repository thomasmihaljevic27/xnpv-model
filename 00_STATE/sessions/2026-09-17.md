# Session 2026-09-17 — correcting the reconciliation

Thomas forwarded an independent review of the valuation integration and the production
reconciliation (`50_REBUILD/docs/Valuation_Integration_Review_Codex.md`, reviewing `bc724e5`). The
integration builds and the raw cross-model comparison reproduces; the reconciliation was not ready
to close. Three findings. All three are now repaired, and two claims from yesterday are withdrawn.

## 1. The "survival effect" was removing the discounting too

Yesterday's report subtracted production's `npv_total` from its `surplus_no_survival` and called
the difference the survival weight. Read in `20_CODE/contract_npv.py`, that column is
**undiscounted** contract surplus plus terminal value at its own reference date. So the
subtraction removed the discounting along with the hazard.

The output said so and it was missed. Removing a survival haircut cannot lower a value when the
value is nonnegative and everything else is held, and the table reported **negative** effects at
seven and eight years. A sign that cannot happen is a stronger signal than a number that looks
plausible, and this one sat in a table I wrote a headline off.

Built `run_production_reconciliation.py`. It imports production's own `NPVEngine`, calls
`npv(player_id, valuation_season)` for each contract to get the per-season detail and the chain of
contract IDs actually valued, and rebuilds the no-hazard value holding everything else:

    no-hazard NPV = sum over seasons of (value - cost) x discount + the original terminal NPV

| term | n | production $M | no hazard $M | the hazard is worth | gap to rebuild | claimed yesterday |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 4 yr | 66 | -4.49 | -3.63 | +0.86 | +5.03 | +0.70 |
| 6 yr | 33 | -14.32 | -12.64 | +1.69 | +15.86 | +0.76 |
| 7 yr | 26 | -21.23 | -19.56 | +1.67 | +24.61 | -0.17 |
| 8 yr | 22 | -28.19 | -25.90 | +2.29 | +33.63 | -0.58 |

Every effect is positive now, zero of 1,141 rows move the wrong way, and the figures match the
reviewer's exactly.

**What that supports is narrower than what I wrote.** The exit hazard alone does not explain the
long-contract gap -- roughly nine-tenths of it survives removing the hazard. It does not locate
the disagreement on the value side: nothing here tests the aging path or the price line, and the
two systems differ in ways that can themselves vary with term. The claim that the pessimism "lives
on the value side" is withdrawn from the report, the standing flag, the work queue and the
decision record.

It also does not test the standing note's actual claim -- that the hazard was offsetting
over-projection against realised outcomes. That needs outcomes, and there are none in a comparison
of two models to each other.

## 2. A shared contract ID is not the same asset at the same date

Production's sweep calls its engine with a player and a season, then labels the result with the
contract ID the loop **asked** about. The engine follows a chain and can value a different
contract. Ten of the 1,141 joined rows do exactly that: Nate Schmidt's six-year 4798 is priced as
one season of 3632, Evander Kane's 7108 as 4118.

Each test is now run and reported separately, because they overlap and the reasons matter more
than the count:

| test | rows failing |
|---|---:|
| production valued a different contract than requested | 10 |
| different number of seasons | 3 |
| production carries terminal control value the rebuild excludes | 185 |
| cost disagrees by more than 10% | 46 |
| valuation year differs from signing year | 240 |
| **comparable on every test** | **912 of 1,141** |

Every row keeps its place in `production_reconciliation.csv` with the flags and a written reason.
Nothing was dropped silently and no production input was changed.

The attrition falls almost entirely on short deals: all 185 terminal-value rows are one- to
three-year contracts, so four, five, seven and eight years lose nobody and the term gradient is
untouched. Six years loses two on cost.

The cost disagreements are a source inconsistency rather than anything the comparison introduced.
The supplied season spine carries a cap hit an order of magnitude from the contract's own AAV on a
handful of deals -- 5606 at $19.216M against $1.866M, 6216 at $8.625M against $0.750M -- and
production prices the cap hit.

Two framings in yesterday's report were also wrong. The two sides are **not** both discounted from
the signing: production values from 1 July of the first contract season, so they sit on different
information dates even when the calendar years agree. And the term gradient, while strong, is not
the whole disagreement: term-group means account for **84.8%** of the squared variation in the
dollar gap.

## 3. The integration's guards accepted inputs that were wrong

The reviewer drove the actual runner with in-memory mutations and it accepted all three: $1M added
to every simulated point surplus, a duplicated production contract (writing 1,218 rows with a
duplicate ID), and sensitivity inputs re-dated into the reserved 2022 cohort.

`run_valuation_integration.py` v1.2 now checks required columns on both inputs, checks for
duplicate keys before joining, uses explicit merge cardinalities with a final uniqueness
assertion, refuses a reserved cohort in this consumer rather than relying on the upstream runner,
compares the simulation's point surplus against the market comparison's adopted surplus to within
$1 (so two artifacts from different runs cannot be joined silently), and names missing IDs instead
of treating `min(n_sens, n_sim)` as evidence the populations agree.

It also stops decomposing anything out of production's nominal column, which is now carried under
an honest name: `production_surplus_nominal_no_survival`.

## What this changes about how to read the reconciliation

The headline that survives is the one about movement: the two systems rank contracts substantially
alike within a term (0.69-0.89 from three to eight years) and disagree about the level on long
contracts, by up to $33.6M a deal at eight years. What does not survive is any statement about
*where* that disagreement comes from. Isolating the hazard rules out one candidate and leaves
several standing, including the definitional and dating differences above.

# Session 2026-09-17b — the reconciliation repair, verified and finished

The reviewer verified `1a6ba0d`: the corrected hazard effects reproduce contract by contract to
within $0.000000004 of an independent calculation, the exception counts reproduce, and all three
integration mutations are now rejected. Two items remained, and "all three findings closed" was
ahead of the implementation. Both are now fixed.

## 1. The corrected calculation still mixed two vintages

The reconciler called production's engine and got fresh season details **and a fresh summary** —
then computed the hazard effect from the fresh details and the **saved spine's** total and
terminal NPVs. Those agree in every run so far, so the published figures are valid. They agree
only while the spine is current.

The reviewer added $1M to the saved totals alone, leaving the engine untouched. The runner
accepted it and produced **1,043 negative hazard effects** — the exact impossible sign that
started this whole correction — and printed the count without failing. A count nothing reads is
how the confounded version survived a run in the first place; printing it again was not a fix.

v1.2 takes `npv_total` and `npv_terminal` from the engine's own summary, carries the spine's total
only to be checked, and asserts two things before any table is built:

- the saved spine agrees with the engine to within a dollar, naming the offenders if not, so a
  stale spine stops the run instead of quietly re-dating the comparison;
- no contract's value falls when the hazard is removed. Production prices a season as
  `(S × value − cost) × discount`, so removing survival moves the value by
  `sum((1 − S) × value × discount)`, nonnegative whenever every season's value is — and the
  minimum season value is now checked on the sample rather than assumed.

Both were tested by deliberately breaking them. Mutating the saved spine fails on all 1,141
contracts with a stale-spine message. Halving one contract's season values in the detail only —
which leaves the spine check passing — fails with the hazard message naming contract 3702. Neither
assertion is vacuous.

## 2. "912 comparable on every test" was not accurate

The code deliberately leaves the date out of its pass mark, and then the report called the result
comparable on every test. It is not:

| screen | contracts |
|---|---:|
| the same asset (identity, season count, cost, terminal value) | 912 |
| of those, the valuation year differs from the signing year | 217 |
| the same asset **and** the years agree | 695 |

And even the 695 are not on the same information date, because production values from 1 July of
the first contract season whatever the year says. The flag is renamed `asset_ok`, with `date_ok`
and `asset_and_date_ok` beside it in the artifact.

The dating difference is not evenly spread. It falls hardest exactly where the disagreement is
largest: **17 of the 22 eight-year contracts** and 12 of the 26 seven-year ones carry it. On the
strictest screen the eight-year cell falls to five contracts, below the reporting threshold — the
runner now says so in the table rather than dropping the row in silence, because a cell too small
to report is itself the finding, and quoting the looser screen instead would be choosing the
number that survives.

Excluding the 217 would not fix any of this. It would hide how unevenly the dating difference
falls.
