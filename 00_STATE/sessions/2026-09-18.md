# Session 2026-09-18 — the goalie comparison, corrected

Three findings on yesterday's goalie bake-off. All three are real, and the first reverses the
headline.

## 1. I compared production against a lookalike of production

I rebuilt production's goalie cascade from the code and called the result "production's rule". It
was not. Three mechanisms were missing:

- production's lookup table has **no games filter** — a two-game season is a prior like any other,
  where mine dropped anything under ten games;
- the cascade fills the t−1, t−2, t−3 slots **strictly**. A goaltender with no t−1 season does not
  fall back to a 60/40 of whatever else exists, which is what mine did; he goes to a **stale anchor**
  computed at an earlier standpoint, bounded three seasons back;
- that stale-anchor population shrinks toward **0.650**, not the league average.

The third I had never read. I read the first half of `shrunk_projection`, stopped at the cascade,
and described the whole method — which is the one thing CLAUDE.md names explicitly: *don't describe
a mechanism you haven't read the code for*. I had the rule in front of me and quoted half of it.

The gap is not cosmetic. The lookalike is **0.094 WAR worse** than the real projector overall, and
up to **2.28 WAR** apart on a single goaltender. Everything I concluded from beating it is gone.

The candidate now imports `GoalieProjector` and calls it — the pattern the qualifying-offer check
already used, which I had built two days ago and then did not apply here. The reimplementation
stays in the bake-off, labelled "a simplified cascade", because the gap between it and the real
thing is itself informative.

## 2. The bootstrap paired forecasts from different years

`paired_bootstrap` joined on goaltender and horizon and left out the page, so a 2015 forecast was
matched against a 2021 one for the same goaltender. 3,683 intended pairs became 19,853 rows, and
the weighting drifted toward goaltenders who appear on many pages. Workload weighting came out at
78% of resamples; correctly paired against the real benchmark, 0%.

A pairing bug is the same class of error as the shared random stream last week: a join key that is
one column short is a silent reweighting, not a crash.

## 3. The ageing candidate never used age

`np.polyfit(x, y, 1)[1]` is the **intercept**. I took it, called it the age slope, and applied one
common drift to every goaltender. Adding twenty years to every subject moved the forecast by
exactly zero — which the reviewer tested and I had not.

So the sign flip I reported and built a standing flag on was in the drift, not in ageing.

## Where the numbers land

| rule | MAE | bias | beats production |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| league average | 1.634 | +0.092 | 0% |
| **production's own projector** | **1.488** | +0.101 | — |
| a simplified cascade (the old "production") | 1.582 | +0.218 | 0% |
| same on the page's own average | 1.511 | +0.045 | 1% |
| same, kept weight fitted (0.43) | 1.491 | **+0.036** | 34% |
| shrunk by games behind | 1.585 | +0.164 | 0% |
| with a fitted age slope | 1.552 | +0.034 | 0% |

**Production's projector wins, and my improvement claim is withdrawn.** The closest candidate is
0.003 WAR worse and takes a third of resamples: a tie.

**The bias result survives and is worth keeping separate.** Production runs +0.101 high at every
horizon; the fitted-weight rule runs +0.036 at the same error. For a forecast that gets multiplied
by a price line and summed over eight seasons, a standing bias is a different kind of defect from
noise — noise averages out over a contract and a roster, a bias does not. That goes into the
price-line decision as a known defect rather than a reason to replace the rule.

**On ageing, the corrected fit says something stronger than the withdrawn claim.** Two coefficients,
and only one is about age:

| page | per year of age | common drift |
|---|---:|---:|
| 2015 | −0.0479 | +0.1167 |
| 2017 | −0.0260 | +0.0792 |
| 2019 | −0.0711 | −0.0019 |
| 2021 | −0.0722 | −0.1057 |

The age slope is **negative on every page**. The drift is what swings. So there is no basis for
"ageing is unidentified here", and the flag is rewritten. Using the slope still does not beat
production (+0.065, 0%), which is a statement about this implementation on 82 seasons a year — not
about whether goaltenders age. The selection concern stands and is untested.

## The pattern in these three

All three are the same shape: a comparison that looked clean because the thing being compared was
not what the label said. A benchmark that was a lookalike, a pairing that matched forecasts across
years, a coefficient that was not the one named. None of my thirty checks could see any of it,
because each tested that the machinery ran, not that it was pointed at the right thing.

The four new checks all test identity rather than behaviour: the benchmark **is** production's
class, the two rules **differ**, one forecast **is** one (page, goaltender, horizon), and a
candidate that says it uses age **moves** when age moves.
