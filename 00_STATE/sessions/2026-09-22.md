# Session 2026-09-22 — the goalie price line, corrected

The review of the price-line pass found two conclusions ahead of the evidence and three smaller
errors. All five were right. The experiment stays; the claims narrow.

## 1. I skipped the simpler answer

I compared no goaltender terms against a goaltender level **and** slope, saw the error fall by a
quarter, and credited the gain to both — then wrote that goaltenders need both, that this was "one
market with two terms", and that it settled the open part of D7 for goaltenders.

The comparison had a missing middle. On the same 174 contracts:

| line | mean abs error | better than the line above |
|---|---:|---:|
| no goaltender terms | 0.010318 | — |
| goaltender level only | **0.007457** | 100% of goaltender-resamples |
| level and slope | 0.007489 | 10% |

The level carries all of it. The slope has not earned its place. That is not the same as the slopes
being equal — 174 contracts cannot tell a small difference from none — and it certainly does not
settle D7. The specification is provisional while the participation model is built, because that
model changes the forecast this line prices.

The general form is worth a standing flag, and now has one: when a richer specification beats a
poorer one, the intermediate step has to be tested before the extra terms are credited.

## 2. "Dollars per win" was a partial slope

The coefficient on the season-average forecast holds first-year production fixed, and for a
restricted player leaves out the restricted interaction. A better player's first year moves too, so
that coefficient is not what a better player is worth. Defined explicitly on the last fit:

| the change | skater $M | goalie $M | ratio |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| partial slope, first year held (UFA) | 0.820 | 0.965 | 1.18 |
| one more win every season, UFA | 2.022 | 2.167 | 1.07 |
| one more win every season, RFA | 1.928 | 2.073 | 1.08 |

The goaltender gap is the same $0.145M in every row because it is one coefficient, so the ratio is
entirely a function of which change is being priced. These are changes in the fitted annual price
before the floor; they are not contract values and the report says so beside the table.

A coefficient is not a price until the change it prices is defined. That is also a standing flag now.

## 3. Three smaller corrections

- **The sample** is 263 eligible → 205 with a forecast → 174 priced out of sample. I had quoted 266,
  a count taken before the census applies its signing-date rule. The runner now prints the attrition
  stage by stage so the figure cannot be quoted from the wrong stage again.
- **The window.** I wrote that the goalie-only line uses an expanding window "rather than" the
  quarterly one. Every line here uses the same scheme: refitted at quarterly cutoffs on an expanding
  sample of contracts signed before the cutoff. My sentence described a difference that does not exist.
- **The +0.167 WAR participation figure** in the forecast report is an illustration of the
  participation term's scale — it gives every played season the same 1.89 WAR — not a decomposition
  of how much of the observed bias participation caused. I had written "that gap alone would produce
  more than the whole observed bias", which reads as a decomposition. Re-worded in the runner, the
  report and the state files.

# Session 2026-09-22b — the goalie participation model

The price-line corrections closed and the next item opened: whether a goaltender plays at all, and
how much he plays if he does, as two separate problems. Ability is held at production's projector
in every arm so the comparison is about participation and role alone.

## Reuse, not copies

The skater participation model is generic apart from one assumption, so it is reused. It needed
trailing anchors, and the anchor builder hard-coded the six skater components; it now takes a
`cols` argument, and the goalie branch passes `["WAR"]`. The skater anchors come out byte-identical
through the default path, which is asserted.

## The first run failed, and the failure is the finding

The first participation fit predicted about 0.87 at every horizon against an observed rate falling
from 0.72 to 0.37, and scored far worse than the flat rate it was meant to replace. A model that
bad is usually wired wrong rather than simply poor, so I measured before reporting anything. The
empirical played rate on the fit's own anchors was right (0.73 → 0.51 → 0.36). The fit's base rate
was 0.90 at every horizon, so it was keeping a different population from the one it was given.

The cause: the skater model drops rows with no age, and a goaltender's birthdate comes mostly from
the contract export. So having one means he was still playing in the contract era.

| goalie anchors | played 0 out | 3 out | 5 out |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| with a birthdate | 0.908 | 0.902 | 0.894 |
| without one | 0.557 | 0.257 | 0.119 |

Dropping ageless rows fitted survivors only. Worse, I had put a has-a-birthdate flag in the share
model as a feature, which means a column recording who was still around years later. For skaters
coverage is 98% and the drop barely matters. For goaltenders it's about half, and that half is
mostly the survivors.

Age is now excluded from both goalie models, and experience from the panel stands in. Check 34
asserts the fit keeps every anchor it is given: its base rate at each horizon has to equal those
anchors' own played rate. Putting age back opens a 0.53 gap, which the check catches.

This also qualifies the bake-off. Its age slope, negative on every page, was fitted on goaltenders
with a birthdate, which is exactly this survivor subsample. The goalie-ageing flag now says so.

The general lesson is now a standing flag: whether a join finds a record can itself be the outcome.

## What the corrected models show

**Whether he plays:** predicted 0.580 against 0.553 observed (the flat rate said 0.641). Brier
0.2075 against 0.2470, lower in 100% of goaltender-resamples. The gain is in the first three
horizons; from three out it roughly ties the flat rate.

**How much, if he plays:** the share model beats the flat carry on the 2,035 played seasons (0.1686
against 0.1801, 100%).

**The season:** participation model plus trailing share takes WAR error from 1.488 to **1.437**
(100%). The share model makes it *worse* (1.581), and the mechanism is clear by role tercile. Backup
bias goes from +0.13 to +0.54; starters barely move. Production's projector gives a season total
shrunk toward a starter-level 2.19. Dividing that by a backup's small trailing share inflates his
implied rate, and a correctly higher share forecast multiplies the inflation. Production's number
cannot be split into rate × share. The share model needs a goalie **rate** forecast, which is next.

**The pooled bias didn't move** (+0.100 → +0.104) even though participation is now roughly right.
That confirms the review's point about the +0.167: it was an illustration, and the bias comes from
somewhere else.

## The price line on the updated forecast

Rerun as requested. Every line is more accurate on the same 174 contracts. The level still accounts
for the improvement (100%), and the slope moves from a loss to a coin flip (58%). The notable
result is instability: the average goalie forecast moved only −0.003 WAR, yet the whole-path UFA
ratio went from **1.07 to 0.75**. The participation model reorders goaltenders without moving the
mean, and the slope estimate follows the reordering. That's a second reason it hasn't earned its
place. The specification stays provisional, and D7 is not settled.

# Session 2026-09-22d — repairing the goalie participation review

The review kept the participation work and asked for four things before the stage closes: date the
contract state at the signing, strengthen the test, correct the bias claim, and reconcile the numbers.
All four are done. Chasing the last one turned up a defect that also moves a skater result.

## 1. Why two runs disagreed

The review's rerun got Brier 0.2101 and WAR error 1.446 against my 0.2075 and 1.437, concentrated at
two seasons out. Both machines were deterministic on their own, so the difference was between them.

The cause is a **singular design**. When every goaltender the contract export knows about is also
under contract for the season, "under contract" is exactly "1 minus contract status unknown", and the
logistic regression has one column too many. On this machine one such fit (2018 page, one season out)
reported convergence with an arbitrary +6.88 / −3.99 split between the two columns, and another
(2019, two out) raised a singular-matrix error and fell back to fewer features. Which branch runs
depends on floating-point details, so another machine can take the other one on the same rows. That
the reviewer's machine did is **inferred** from where the gap sits, not observed.

Fix: `participation_model.py` v1.5 drops redundant columns in a fixed order (`_full_rank`, order
starting with contract status unknown) until the design has full rank. Every machine now makes the
same choice. Corrected goalie results: participation Brier **0.2056** (flat 0.2470, 100% of
resamples), predicted 0.575 against 0.553 observed; season WAR error 1.488 -> **1.432**; bias +0.096.

## 2. The same defect in the skater model

Fifteen skater fits in the contract-using variants (2015–2019 pages, one to five seasons out) were
singular. The arbitrary split fitted the training rows and over-predicted on the page — 0.556
against 0.406 observed on the 2019 page, five out. That is where Phase 2's "contract data makes the
participation forecast worse" came from. New runner `run_contract_ablation.py` measures it on one
harness, with the old behaviour reproduced by switching the rank rule off:

| seasons out | worse WAR error with contracts, before the fix | after the fix |
|---|---:|---:|
| 0 | +0.03% | +0.03% |
| 1 | +0.45% | +0.08% |
| 2 | +0.89% | +0.27% |
| 3 | +0.98% | +0.06% |
| 4 | +0.81% | +0.12% |
| 5 | +0.90% | +0.03% |

Participation Brier: 0.1340 without contracts, 0.1354 with them before the fix, 0.1326 after. The
"hurts" finding is withdrawn in conclusion (correction section in `Phase2_Participation.md`). The
standing leader fits without contract data and had no singular fits, so its own numbers are
unaffected — but the choice was partly justified by the withdrawn finding. **Left open for Thomas**:
with contracts is now a near tie on WAR and slightly better on participation.

## 3. Contract state read at the signing

`run_goalie_price_line.py` v2.2 dates participation's contract inputs at each contract's signing
rather than 1 July. 95 of 205 contracts change (the review counted 92 with its training fit held
