## What was repaired

Item 1, the shortened-season units. `player_season_table.py` scaled the season total to an
82-game basis under the locked D20 decision and then built the per-82 rate by dividing that
already-scaled total by the player's own games, so the schedule adjustment was applied twice.
The fix computes the rate from the raw total and applies D20 after the aggregation instead of
before it. D20 is unchanged in substance: a shortened season's total is still stated on an
82-game basis. Since the scaling is 82 over the schedule and the games share divides by the
same schedule, the two now cancel exactly, and the identity holds on all 17,050 played rows.
The 61 rows whose source-merged trade halves carry more games than the schedule are excluded
by the games-share clip, counted rather than hidden.

The effect is larger on the inputs than on the reconstruction. The 2020-21 median rate falls
from 0.9319 to 0.6364, against a full-length-season range of 0.4485 to 0.6282, so rates from
the two shortened seasons are now on the same scale as the rest and the trailing anchors that
blend across them are no longer mixing scales.

Also in item 1: the harness converted a predicted games share on a flat 82-game basis while
scoring it against raw games in a shortened season, which charged a player available for all 56
games of 2020-21 a 26-game error. Games are now converted on the outcome season's own schedule.

Item 2, the guards. Four, each of which fails on the code as it stood. A model now refuses a
horizon it never fitted, in place of returning a flat 0.6 participation and the trailing rate.
The participation model refuses the same, and the fitted range is named once in
`rebuild_config.FITTED_HORIZONS` rather than hardcoded in seven places. The harness validates
the exact requested player-by-horizon grid before scoring, so a model that answers for one
player on a page of 866 is refused instead of being scored on the sample it chose. The
named-player runner requests the whole term instead of silently repricing ten years as eight.

The holdout work, brought forward at the reviewer's request. Reserved market start cohorts are
now enforced in both market runners alongside the existing page seal, the page seal now
requires a written reason rather than accepting an empty one, and every inspection appends to a
committed ledger. `50_REBUILD/docs/Holdout_Inventory.md` records the state of both reserved
samples: the forecast holdout is intact on the evidence of the code and the reports, and the
market holdout is spent, because both market runners swept start years through 2025 and
selected on each year they touched.

**Item 4, the participation event.** The one games threshold was answering three different
questions. It still decides whether a past season is usable evidence, at ten games. The event

condition on the same event, so the two halves of the forecast are finally about the same thing.
On the previous commit this check fails and names the damage: 2,170 training pairs carried a

Moving to one game admits 2,918 cameo seasons, 17.1% of rows and -0.10% of total WAR, whose
per-82 rates run from -8.4 to +41.5 because dividing by one to nine games and multiplying by 82
amplifies noise up to eighty times. Rate fits are therefore weighted by games played, which is
the precision weight rather than a convenience: a rate's sampling variance scales as one over
the games behind it. **This is the decision the review left open and it is flagged, not locked.**
The alternative it named, modelling short appearances as their own low-production state, remains
available and is the larger change.

**Item 4, eligibility.** The window said three seasons and the trailing level stopped at two, so
a player whose only qualifying season was the oldest in the window was dropped. On the 2021 page
that is 120 careers, exactly the count the review predicted. They are admitted with a
history tier recorded on each row, and they get an anchor from their most recent qualifying
season through a deep-lookback fallback that adds only the rows the window was missing. The
three-season models are unaffected by construction, and the check confirms it: the adopted
leader's existing predictions move by 8.9e-16. The two-season benchmark moves by 1.5e-02,
because it legitimately gains 5,703 stale training pairs. Two-season anchors plus the stale
rows reach exactly the 20,068 the three-season window already had.

Age bands are now defined at the valuation season rather than in the last season played, so the
subgroup populations are defined at the forecast date. The models were always aged correctly;
this was the reporting side of the same quantity.

## Item 5: the market is dated at the signing, and the dollars are discounted

**The cameo weighting is settled.** Thomas took the games-weighted option, so the rate fits carry
it and the alternative low-production state is closed rather than pending.

**The fitted range now depends on the page, because the evidence does.** The six-horizon limit
turned out not to be a choice: a training pair at horizon h needs an outcome completed before
the valuation season, so at the 2015 page horizon 6 has 270 pairs and horizon 7 has none at all,
while at 2021 horizon 7 has 1,246 and the range reaches 11. Each model now records what its own
page supported and refuses past it. Horizons 7 and 8 at the 2018 page carry 177 and 176 distinct
fitted probabilities spanning 0.005 to 0.95, where every player used to receive exactly 0.6.

**Where a contract outruns its page, the extrapolation is declared and tested.** 28 of 1,927
development contracts need a horizon their page cannot reach, 1.5%, and all of them are long
deals, which is the population that matters most. The first rule tried was to hold the last
fitted season flat. Measured against pages where those horizons are genuinely fitted, that
overstates production by 36%, 88% and 170% one, two and three seasons past the range, so it was
discarded. The rule that ships continues the decay observed at the end of the fitted range,
measured league-wide on the product and split so that participation keeps its own rate. That
leaves +3.0%, +10.0% and +19.8% on average and +32% on the worst page at three seasons out.
Measuring a ratio per component and applying both was also tried and undershoots by 21% to 45%,
because it decays the product twice. Every extrapolated row is tagged.

The residual bias is up and it is not corrected. Dividing it out would mean three tuned
constants repairing 1.5% of the sample. The direction is stated instead: a contract priced
partly on extrapolation is flattered, mildly and knowably.

**Market fits are dated at the signing.** `ProductionCurrency.fit()` takes a date, refuses a
season outright, and asserts that the latest training signing precedes it. The two rolling
runners and the surplus runner all train on contracts signed before the test contract's own
signing quarter rather than before its start year. The module docstring claimed signing-dated
fits before this was true of the fit; it is true now.

**The cap path is dated too, and both sides are discounted.** `cap_path()` uses a ceiling only
once announced, normally the June before the season and 2025-01-31 for the three seasons
published together, and grows at 3% beyond. Seen from 2017 the 2020 ceiling is 82.0M against the
81.5M it turned out to be, so a historical valuation no longer knows the flat cap before it was
announced. Value and cost are discounted on the same schedule. Under locked decision D24 the 3%
growth and 3% discount cancel in cap-share terms, and the check demonstrates that identity
rather than assuming it.

**A gap left open deliberately.** The announced 2026-27 and 2027-28 ceilings are public but are
not in this repository's source data, so they are not in `CAP_CEILING` and those seasons
extrapolate at 3% instead, which understates them. Entering a number from memory into a thesis
model is not worth the convenience. Recorded in the config where it will be found.

The whole chain runs end to end: 1,226 contracts priced on development cohorts, each on a line
fitted only to deals signed before it. The check suite is at 11 of 11, and every check fails on
the code that preceded it.

## Item 6: the live chain is now a comparator, and it changes the headline

**The adapter runs production's own code.** `50_REBUILD/code/production_adapter.py` imports the
locked aging curve, the D3 decay path with its D21 age-1 basing, the exit-hazard table and the
survival convention from the production modules rather than reimplementing any of them. A second
copy of a rule drifting from the first has already cost this project twice, so nothing is
restated here.

`SkaterProjector.__init__` reads the contract season spine, which is built from scraped clause
data this checkout does not have. The spine is about contracts and the forecast is about
players, so the projector is built through `__new__` and given only the attributes the
projection path actually uses, constructed from production's own modules. Running a scrape to
satisfy an import that the imported code never reads was the alternative.

The mapping is where the honesty sits. The rate is production's projection, the anchor walked
along the locked decay path. Games share is 1.0, which is not an omission in the adapter but
production's own conflation of level with availability. Participation is the exit-hazard
survival factor, accumulated on production's convention of reading the hazard at the previous
season's age and quality. Production's documented limitations travel with it, the curve fitted
on the whole panel included, because repairing them would stop it being production.

One adapter bug worth recording because it was nearly invisible: the curve indexes an age array,
so passing 27.0 instead of 27 raised inside it, `ratio_path` swallowed the error as "no
qualifying season", and every player came back on a flat path. The adapter would have looked
exactly like the benchmark it exists to replace. 599 of 986 subjects now take the curve path.

**The correct comparator halves the headline and removes its shape.**

By age band against the live chain: 2.6% at 22 and under, 5.9% at 23-26, 16.6% at 27-30, 27.5%

**The mislabelling is corrected everywhere it appears**, not only in prose. The benchmark class
