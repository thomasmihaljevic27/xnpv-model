## What was repaired

Item 1, the shortened-season units. `player_season_table.py` scaled the season total to an
82-game basis under the locked D20 decision and then built the per-82 rate by dividing that
already-scaled total by the player's own games, so the schedule adjustment was applied twice.
The fix computes the rate from the raw total and applies D20 after the aggregation instead of
before it. D20 is unchanged in substance: a shortened season's total is still stated on an
82-game basis. Since the scaling is 82 over the schedule and the games share divides by the
same schedule, the two now cancel exactly, and the identity holds on all 17,050 played rows.
The 61 rows whose source-merged trade halves carry more games than the schedule are excluded
by the games-share clip, counted rather than hidden.

The effect is larger on the inputs than on the reconstruction. The 2020-21 median rate falls
from 0.9319 to 0.6364, against a full-length-season range of 0.4485 to 0.6282, so rates from
the two shortened seasons are now on the same scale as the rest and the trailing anchors that
blend across them are no longer mixing scales.

Also in item 1: the harness converted a predicted games share on a flat 82-game basis while
scoring it against raw games in a shortened season, which charged a player available for all 56
games of 2020-21 a 26-game error. Games are now converted on the outcome season's own schedule.

Item 2, the guards. Four, each of which fails on the code as it stood. A model now refuses a
horizon it never fitted, in place of returning a flat 0.6 participation and the trailing rate.
The participation model refuses the same, and the fitted range is named once in
`rebuild_config.FITTED_HORIZONS` rather than hardcoded in seven places. The harness validates
the exact requested player-by-horizon grid before scoring, so a model that answers for one
player on a page of 866 is refused instead of being scored on the sample it chose. The
named-player runner requests the whole term instead of silently repricing ten years as eight.

The holdout work, brought forward at the reviewer's request. Reserved market start cohorts are
now enforced in both market runners alongside the existing page seal, the page seal now
requires a written reason rather than accepting an empty one, and every inspection appends to a
committed ledger. `50_REBUILD/docs/Holdout_Inventory.md` records the state of both reserved
samples: the forecast holdout is intact on the evidence of the code and the reports, and the
market holdout is spent, because both market runners swept start years through 2025 and
selected on each year they touched.

## Limits, and they are real

This container has the vendor WAR file but not the confidential contract export, so there are
no birthdates and no ages, and every age-dependent fit degenerates here. That is an environment
limit and not a regression. It means the units repair is demonstrated on the table and the
guards are demonstrated structurally, while the refit that has to follow, the participation
fits, and everything on the market side cannot be run on this machine.

Nothing was refit. Each dollar figure in the candidate reports stays withdrawn. Items 3 to 5,
which are the participation event and eligibility, the market dating, and the cap path with
discounting, are not started. The production adapter and the report and log corrections that go
with it are not started. Published figures that depend on the two shortened seasons will move
when the chain is refit, and no estimate of by how much is offered here.

## Later the same session: the vendor exports arrived, and item 4

Thomas supplied the two confidential PuckPedia exports, so the chain runs end to end here. They
sit in `10_SOURCE/` under their expected names and are ignored by the `*CONFIDENTIAL*` rule; the
working tree stayed clean after they were placed. The production reproduction guard passes, so
this is the right data vintage: the locked skater coefficients come back inside 5e-6.

**Age coverage is 69.2% of season rows here, not the 98.3% on record.** The birthdate join takes
PuckPedia plus an Elite Prospects file that is not in the repository, so early seasons are
thin: 14% in 2007 rising to 99% by 2018, and 93.8% on the 2015-and-later pages. Every figure
below is depressed by that and none of them should be compared with a published number.

**The signing-date claim is confirmed independently.** 3,550 eligible standard-level skater
contracts starting 2015-2025, 3,550 parseable signing dates, none missing. No fallback rule is
needed. The dating exposure reproduces too: the 2022 start-year pool contains 354 contracts
signed after the earliest 2022 signing, matching the review's largest-pool figure.

**Item 4, the participation event.** The one games threshold was answering three different
questions. It still decides whether a past season is usable evidence, at ten games. The event
the participation model predicts is now the plan's one game, and the rate and games targets
condition on the same event, so the two halves of the forecast are finally about the same thing.
On the previous commit this check fails and names the damage: 2,170 training pairs carried a
rate target while being classified as not played.

Moving to one game admits 2,918 cameo seasons, 17.1% of rows and -0.10% of total WAR, whose
per-82 rates run from -8.4 to +41.5 because dividing by one to nine games and multiplying by 82
amplifies noise up to eighty times. Rate fits are therefore weighted by games played, which is
the precision weight rather than a convenience: a rate's sampling variance scales as one over
the games behind it. **This is the decision the review left open and it is flagged, not locked.**
The alternative it named, modelling short appearances as their own low-production state, remains
available and is the larger change.

**Item 4, eligibility.** The window said three seasons and the trailing level stopped at two, so
a player whose only qualifying season was the oldest in the window was dropped. On the 2021 page
that is 120 careers, exactly the count the review predicted. They are admitted with a
history tier recorded on each row, and they get an anchor from their most recent qualifying
season through a deep-lookback fallback that adds only the rows the window was missing. The
three-season models are unaffected by construction, and the check confirms it: the adopted
leader's existing predictions move by 8.9e-16. The two-season benchmark moves by 1.5e-02,
because it legitimately gains 5,703 stale training pairs. Two-season anchors plus the stale
rows reach exactly the 20,068 the three-season window already had.

Age bands are now defined at the valuation season rather than in the last season played, so the
subgroup populations are defined at the forecast date. The models were always aged correctly;
this was the reporting side of the same quantity.

## The headline claim does not reproduce here, and the repairs are not why

Same model, same harness, same birthdates, three code states, error against the flat benchmark
at five seasons out: **-24.0% before any repair, -24.3% after items 1 to 3, -23.6% after item 4.**
The repairs move it by less than a point, and most of that is item 4 changing the scored sample.
`run_stress_tests.py` on the repaired tree agrees with the harness exactly at -23.6%.

`Phase5_StressTests.md` publishes -43.5%. That figure does not reproduce here on repaired or
unrepaired code, so the repairs did not cause the gap; the missing Elite Prospects birthdates
are the obvious candidate, since the aging model has far less to work with at 69.2% coverage
than at 98.3%. **This needs re-deriving on a machine with the full birthdate join before either
number is quoted.** Nothing here shows the published figure is wrong, only that it is not
reproducible in this environment and that the repairs are not the explanation.

One caveat on the tiered results that matters for reading any before-and-after. The admitted
returners are an easy population: they play at 15.7% and 6.2% by tier against 68.2% for
current-history players, so predicting near zero for them is accurate and their arrival lowers
average error. Figures before and after item 4 are not like-for-like, which is why the decomposition
above holds the model and the harness fixed and reports the sample size at each step.

## The Elite Prospects file arrived, and the flag closed

Thomas supplied `ep_birthdates.csv`. Coverage goes to **98.346%** of season rows, matching the
figure on record to three decimals, so this environment is now the one the published results
came from.

**The published figures reproduce exactly.** On the pre-repair code at full coverage the
improvement over the flat benchmark is -15.8% at the valuation season and -43.5% five seasons
out, which is `Phase5_StressTests.md` to the decimal. Nothing published was wrong and nothing
needs re-deriving. The standing flag raised earlier today is rewritten as resolved rather than
left standing, because the hypothesis in it turned out to be right: a thin age join starves the
aging model, and the shortfall showed up as a shrunken improvement rather than as an error.

Holding the model and harness fixed and varying only the code state, at five seasons out:

| code state | old eligibility rule | all rows |
|---|---|---|
| original, pre-repair | -43.5% (n=5,258) | not applicable |
| items 1 to 3 | -43.5% | not applicable |
| items 1 to 4 | -43.4% (n=5,258) | -46.1% (n=5,930) |

Two readings. On identical rows the repairs move the headline by a tenth of a point at five
seasons out and 1.5 points at the valuation season, so the units, guard, event and weighting
work leaves the case essentially where it was. Admitting the returning players **improves** the
measured advantage, from -43.4% to -46.1%, because they are a population the flat benchmark
handles especially badly: it carries a stale anchor forward at full health for players who go
on to play 6% to 16% of the time, while a participation model correctly predicts close to
nothing. Dropping them was understating the rebuild's case rather than flattering it.

Every figure here is still measured against the flat-carry benchmark. The comparator finding is
untouched by any of this.

## One guard added, from the flag's own lesson

The rebuild had a silent dependency on a file that is not in the repository and whose absence
degraded results without failing anything. `player_season_table.build()` now refuses below 95%
age coverage, naming the missing Elite Prospects file and what the absence costs, with
`allow_thin_ages=True` to proceed deliberately. Verified both ways: the full join passes, the
PuckPedia-only join is refused, the override works.
