- [REQUEST] Thomas: the draft picks and prospects are not discounted in their current form; why?
- [ANSWERED] They are discounted. The pick curve adds up each season's surplus as a share of that season's cap and
  states the total at the 2025-26 cap (`pick_curve.py` step 7; costs divided by the season's cap at line 356). With
  cap growth and the discount rate one constant (3%, D11 and D17), a cap share k seasons out is worth the same share of
  today's cap, so the cap-share sum is the 3% discount written in reduced form. The player model does the same:
  `contract_npv.py` divides by (1.03)^k a value it first grew by (1.03)^k, so only the fixed-dollar cap hit really
  shrinks; the pick curve's costs (entry-level maximum, qualifying offers) are fixed dollars too, divided by each
  season's cap. One difference: the pick curve's seasons are real careers, so it uses each season's actual cap, which
  amounts to discounting at the cap's actual growth. Consequence stated: waiting costs nothing in this unit (a player
  arriving at D+4, a pick two drafts out), on the value side as for players; clubs' impatience is left out of the rate
  on purpose (D15). Caveat stated: the cancellation rests on the rate equalling cap growth; picks and prospects are the
  assets most exposed if it does not (standing flag of 2026-09-14, the growth sweep at 3%, 5%, 7.6%).
- [REQUEST] Thomas: why have a separate age-discounted production term (production x (age - 18)) rather than just
  discounting the existing production terms?
- [ANSWERED] The term is the discount of the linear production term: a point is worth b + e x (age - 18) (D+1:
  b = -$0.075M, e = -$0.075M a point a year). Production squared is not discounted, so a year of age costs a low
  scorer a larger share of production's value than a high scorer (D+1: 54% at 10 points per 82, 21% at 20, on the
  outputs before e1e4f60). The 2026-10-14 age test tried only no age, age alone, production x age, and both; no form
  reaching the square had been tested.
