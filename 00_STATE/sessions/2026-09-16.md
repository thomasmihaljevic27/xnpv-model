# 2026-09-16: taking the coverage gap apart, and finding the participation model in it

The uncertainty repairs were verified clean the night before: all three findings closed, 22
checks passing with no skips, and the reviewer's own adversarial test confirming the new
distribution-mean guard fires when the fix is disabled. What was left was not a bug. It was the
open model question both reviews had now asked in the same words:

> The current evidence does not cleanly separate how much comes from forecasts being too low,
> uncertainty ranges being too narrow, or participation estimates.

That is a well-posed question, and it is also the first item on the queue, so it was the work.

## How it was asked

Three levers, each set to the value the outcomes say it should have had for that subgroup at that
horizon, coverage recomputed with each one alone and with all three: shift the centre until the
group's standardised misses sit where the band's shape says, stretch the band until its realized
span matches, replace the predicted probability of playing with the share who did.

Every one of them uses the answer. That is the point and it is stated three times in the file: it
measures the **ceiling** a perfect repair could reach with hindsight on this sample, which is an
upper bound and not a forecast of a real repair. A lever that cannot close the gap with hindsight
certainly cannot close it without.

Because an oracle says how much a lever could buy and not what is wrong with it, two things were
also measured with no hindsight at all: the participation probability against the realized play
rate, and the season-total bias split into rate, games and participation.

## What came back

**No single lever closes the star gap.** Five seasons out the best of the three closes 36% of the
distance to the stated 80%, and all three together overshoot it. Which one dominates changes with
distance: the centre near the valuation season, the spread and participation further out. The
star gap is three defects, not one, and I had been writing about it as one.

**For players 22 and under the centre is the wrong lever.** Correcting it makes their coverage
*worse* at five of six horizons. The spread carries 47 to 110% and participation 31 to 62%. The
oracle shift for them is near zero at the short horizons, so the forecast is not systematically
low for them where the gap opens. The queue's stated fix for this population — a prior from the
prospect pillar — addresses a component that is not the problem.

**Participation is squeezed toward the middle, and it is the finding of the day.** Predicted
against actual, one direction the whole length of both tables: 22-and-unders 0.677 against 0.804
five seasons out and under-predicted at *every* horizon; 3+ 0.781 against 0.883; 2-to-3 0.682
against 0.799; and the other way, 34-and-overs 0.491 against 0.373 at the valuation season. The
groups that survive are under-predicted and the groups that do not are over-predicted. That is
under-dispersion: the model shrinks every group toward the league.

**The aggregate could not see it.** Predicted 0.752 against 0.710 actual at the valuation season,
0.352 against 0.383 five out — the two halves of the error cancel — and the Brier score sits at
0.126 to 0.138 at every horizon with no trend. A single calibration number over a population
containing both halves of a compensating error is blind to it by construction. Worth remembering
the next time a flat metric is read as reassurance.

**The point bias agrees from the other side.** The stars' bias is almost entirely rate: −0.921 of
a −0.866 total five seasons out, with participation contributing +0.002 at three out. The
22-and-unders at three seasons out are the opposite: rate off by −0.072, total off by −0.340,
participation off by −0.114. A group whose total is badly biased while its rate is not has a
participation problem wearing a forecasting problem's clothes.

## What it changes

Participation moves to the front of the queue. It had been untouched Phase 2 work with no known
defect beyond the population question; it now has a measured, one-directional defect that reaches
both of the populations the model is worst at, and it is the only lever that helps every
under-covered group.

The star residual keeps its place and loses some scope. It is real, it is confirmed twice here,
it is a **rate** defect worth about nine tenths of a win a season five years out for the players
who carry the money — and it closes at most a quarter of the star coverage gap at that horizon.
It should not be sold as the whole of the star problem, which is how this branch was describing
it yesterday.

The band's own tier-or-age spread term comes off the deferred list. It had been parked until the
centre was fixed. For the young it is the largest single lever at every horizon and a centre
repair would not help them, so the deferral was waiting for nothing.

## Limits, said plainly

Every lever is an oracle. The star subgroup is 203 scored rows a horizon and 171 five out, with
far fewer careers behind them, so those figures are the least stable in the report. Levers
substitute for one another and a nonlinear interval does not decompose exactly into three causes,
which is why the overshoot column is printed. Coverage is not accuracy. Nothing in the document
is fitted for use and nothing is in the chain.

## Not touched

No production code. No locked decision. No reserved page. The confirmatory seal is intact.
