## What the cap-dump flag means (Thomas: "Team A wanted to clear cap, so they took on a cheaper contract ... Or Team A wanted to clear cap, so Team B took on that contract?")

- [CHECKED] Read from `backtest_cuts_more.py` cut 8(i) and `groups_clustered.sides()`: a two-team trade is a cap dump when
  one club sent a player at a cap hit of $2.5M or more with at least one pick or prospect and got back no player at $1M or
  more. `t_dump` is 1 for the OTHER club, the one that took the contract on (19 + 4 of the 556 regression trades; never
  both). The label "Took on a contract to clear cap" reads either way; a rewording was offered, not made.

## How the regression's variables are coded (Thomas: "Is it the case that each and every pair of rows (same trades) are exact mirrors of each other?")

- [CHECKED] Each variable is the club's own yes/no (or measure) less the other club's; the second row is the first times
  -1, outcome included (guarded in `one_regression.joint`). Both clubs can hold a trait (rental 36 trades, defenceman 26,
  received a player 162); its difference is then 0 on both rows. Clustering by trade keeps the doubled rows from adding
  evidence: one row a trade with robust standard errors and two rows clustered agree to about 1% (clause 0.498 / 0.493);
  two rows without clustering would give about half the standard error.

  mirrored copies to explain, plain robust standard errors). Not decided.
