- [QUESTION] Thomas asked whether the model understands that a contract holds a flat AAV while value is compared on a cap-share basis, so that a player worth $8M today is worth $8.4M next year under 5% cap growth, and whether a contract signed a year earlier therefore carries surplus against the same contract signed a year later.
- [FINDING] Yes, and the mechanism is `cap_path()` in `skater_forward_projection.py` (lines 381-386): `CAP_CEILING[t0] * (1 + CAP_GROWTH) ** k`, with `val_raw = (ALPHA + _slope * pw) * ceil` at line 727. Value is nominal dollars on a growing ceiling, so a constant cap share converts to a rising dollar figure. Cost is `cs_cap_hit` filled from `pp_cap_hit` (line 523), read per contract-season rather than assumed flat, so ELC bonus years and 35+ cases come through as they are. `contract_npv.py` then discounts at the same constant (`G = CAP_GROWTH`, line 197; `disc = (1 + G) ** (-k)`, line 426). Illustration at g=3%, five years, identical players and terms: a cap hit set a year earlier prices at +$3.463M NPV against +$2.331M for the same cap share priced a year later, a gap of $1.132M, which is five times the $0.24M nominal price difference and does not decay in k.

- [QUESTION] Thomas then argued the discount rate erodes the benefit of signing long term, since part of that benefit is the declining cap share a flat cap hit occupies, and separately that a uniform rate assumes each club values the future identically.
- [FINDING] The first half is the reverse of what the code does, and the correction matters because it is load-bearing. Growth and discount are one constant, so they cancel on the value term (D24) and the surviving `cost / (1+g)^k` is the declining cap share itself. There is no pure time discount in cap-share space: year-8 surplus counts as much as year-1, and the only brakes on term are the exit hazard (D18: 1.1%/yr star, 0.9% regular, against 10.3%/19.4% fringe and below-replacement) and the aging curve. Term is rewarded monotonically, at +$0.23M / +$1.37M / +$6.16M for two, four, and eight years at g=3% on a flat cap hit equal to t0 value.
- [FINDING] The premise that long term lowers AAV is also backwards in this sample. The length specification in `term_premium_test.py` (lines 8-10) carries `c_len = 0.00859642`, or +$0.82M of annual cap hit per extra year of term, controlling for trailing production. Long deals show negative NPV because clubs pay a term premium that Stage 2 found buys no measurable production (main spec f = -0.0135, 95% CI [-0.0360, +0.0089], value-side share 0.000), not because the discounting penalises length.
- [FINDING] **NEW, and the reason for the new standing flag.** The version of the objection that does hold runs through g rather than through discounting. Because the cost-erosion channel runs at exactly g, raising g raises the reward for term: the same eight-year deal moves +$6.16M at 3%, +$9.71M at 5%, and +$13.77M at 7.6%, against realized ceiling growth of 7.6%/yr from 2023-24 to 2026-27. The existing published-ceiling flag records this constant as a level error; it is also a term-gradient error, and that framing makes part of the long-contract negative-NPV finding a candidate g artifact. Stage 2 tested length on the value side and did not test the growth assumption, so this is an untested competing explanation, not a contradiction of the null.
- [FINDING] The second half of the objection is correct and is D15 working as designed: the rate is normative so that club impatience is a back-test finding rather than an input. Two standing flags already cover the substance (club-specific versus league-average value, contender versus rebuilder marginal-win value). The sharper point is that heterogeneous time preference is why trades clear at all, so a rebuilder trading a win-now player to a contender at mutual advantage is efficient gains from trade, and the back-test scores it as mispricing against one league-average yardstick regardless.

- [QUESTION] Thomas asked whether differences in clubs' assumed r could be found by era or build timeline, assuming the rest of the NPV model is sound.
- [FINDING] The sequencing inverts. Identification needs time-profile spread between the two sides of a trade, which lives in the 654 of 946 groups involving a pick (69.1%); the 205 clean player-only trades are the sample with the least of it. Trade-sides per club are roughly 13 on the clean 205 and 59 on all 946, so club-specific rates are not estimable here even though a two-sided fixed-effects decomposition over the trade network is the correct design in principle. Era differences and a club-state covariate are estimable; club effects are not.
- [FINDING] The shelved pure-pick finding does not kill this. It killed a level, because corner solutions under the minimum-denomination floor make equality-based estimation misspecified (per-trade implied values 0.015 to 0.689 around a fitted 0.486/0.510). A corner still yields an inequality, and if the censoring is common across groups it differences out. The forward path already recorded with that item, bounds rather than a point, is the right one, and sweetener size scaling with gap at Spearman 0.735 is the evidence the bounds will be narrow enough to compare.
