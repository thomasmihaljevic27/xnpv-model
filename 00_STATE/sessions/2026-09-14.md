# Session 2026-09-14 - Elite players and the aging curve's comparable weighting

- [CONTEXT] Thomas raised two linked questions about why elite players are structurally awkward for the model. (1) Is the position-and-age average doing more harm than good, i.e. does the curve let too little player-specific performance into the estimate? (2) Specifically, is the fixed pooled weight of ten deflating elite players, because elite players have fewer comparables and so a smaller similarity-weight sum? He quoted the passage in `40_DOCS/Supervisor_Drafts/Data, Production, and Aging.docx` that illustrates the split with comparable weights summing to twenty, then to five.
- [FINDING] Read from `aging_curve.py` rather than the explainers. Both shrinkage steps in the anchor were measured with a new read-only audit. **Question 2 is measurably not the problem. Question 1 is, and by more than the question assumed.**
- [FINDING] The pooled weight of ten is worth about **4-5% of the estimate at every level band**, elite included (median similarity-weight sum 190-240 against a fixed ten). The explainer's illustrative sums of twenty and five imply a pooled share of a third to two-thirds, roughly an order of magnitude above the real regime, and they point a reader at exactly the wrong lever. Elite players do **not** carry deflated weight sums: their median sum is 194 against 236 for the mid band, and their pooled share, 0.049, is indistinguishable from the negative band's 0.050.
- [FINDING] The real lever is the **bandwidth**. `h` is the median within-position pairwise distance of the whole cloud (2.526, matching the value in the production docstring), so the Gaussian kernel is nearly flat and barely selects. Measured retention slope — the share of a player's deviation from his position-and-age cell that survives into the comparable estimate — is **0.193 for forwards and 0.167 for defencemen**, falling only to 0.180 / 0.151 after the pooled weight is applied. The anchor therefore keeps `LAMBDA + (1-LAMBDA) x slope` = **0.631 of a forward's deviation, and 0.637 is all that setting SHRINK_K to zero could buy.** Six tenths of one percentage point.
- [FINDING] The comparable set is not comparable at the top. For a median elite target at 3.73 WAR/82 the comparable estimate lands at **1.58**; only **7% of the comparable weight** comes from players within half a win per 82 of him (37% for the `lo` band); the Kish effective number of comparables is **369 against a pool of 460**, so the kernel is close to an unweighted cohort average. Nineteen genuinely similar players sit in that pool and carry 7% of the weight.
- [FINDING] **The deflation does not cancel in the ratio, it is amplified by it.** `skater_forward_projection.ratio_path()` takes `base = levels[0]`, the mean-reverted level, and prices off `l / base`, then applies that ratio to the player's unshrunk 60/40 raw-WAR anchor. The forward steps in the numerator are age-conditional deltas in absolute WAR units and do not scale with the level. A shrunken denominator with a same-sized numerator increment therefore **steepens the projected percentage decline** for exactly the players whose anchors are shrunk most, compounding across the back half of the long contracts elite players sign. The module docstring's "we take only its SHAPE ... the units stay regression-native" holds only if the deltas scale with the level, and they do not.
- [FINDING] Where the pooled weight of ten **does** bite is thin **age** cells, not high **level** cells — already measured in `40_DOCS/Aging_Curve_Coverage_Audit.md` Finding 4 (league-curve share 78% at 19, 31% at 20, 49% at 37). Elite players on eight-year deals age into that thin end, so the pooled term and the ratio amplification meet in the back half of a max-term contract. That interaction is untested.
- [FINDING] `SHRINK_K` is also a divide-by-zero guard, not only a weighting choice. `_shrunk` masks candidate weights to those with an observed value at the age being estimated, so the sum is exactly zero whenever no comparable has one — routine for the delta column at the oldest ages. At `SHRINK_K = 0` that is 0/0 and the projection returns NaN. Found by running it; the test arm uses an epsilon instead, so it tests removing the pooling rather than removing the guard.
- [DONE] Two new scripts, neither touching a production constant or a file the chain reads.
  - `20_CODE/aging_comp_weight_audit.py` v1.0 — read-only. Reports the fitted bandwidth, the retention slope by position before and after pooling, and comparable-set quality by level band (share of weight from similar players, Kish effective comparables, pooled share). Writes `30_OUTPUT/aging_comp_weight_audit.csv` with per-age detail, since the band table pools the age effect away.
  - `20_CODE/aging_elite_shrinkage_test.py` v1.0 — nine-arm held-out comparison on the bandwidth test's design: production, three `SHRINK_K` settings, three bandwidth scalings, the joint cell that `40_DOCS/Aging_Yardstick_Comparison.md` records as untested, and a reliability-weighted lambda (`n/(n+n0)`, with `n0` solved on **training** careers so the arm reproduces lambda = 0.55 at a median-length record and only redistributes). Reports **signed bias within fixed anchor-level bands** on both the curve and the season-total endpoint, because pooled MAE is dominated by the middle and hides a tail-confined bias — neither the production `validate()` table nor the bandwidth test reports it. Reuses `truncated_player` and `ratio_prediction` from `aging_bandwidth_test.py` by import rather than by copy, so the two tests cannot drift on holdout semantics.

## Open threads

- Run both scripts on the desktop machine against the real `WAR_with_age.csv`. The audit takes seconds; the nine-arm test is the bandwidth test's cost with more projection arms.
- The nine-arm test sweeps many arms against one evaluation set. A winning arm is a lead to re-test on the historical-window mode, not a result — and any change to the bandwidth rule or lambda is a deliberate revisit of a locked decision, with a lambda re-test and full-chain movement, on the terms the coverage audit already set out.
- The fat-tailed-prior argument is the version of Thomas's question that survives contact with the code: a constant retention is optimal under a Gaussian prior, and NHL talent is plainly not Gaussian at the top. Whether to shrink on a transformed scale, use a fat-tailed prior, or cap the pull with a limited-translation rule is undecided, and the reliability-lambda arm is the level-blind version that is hardest to mistake for tail-fitting.
- `40_DOCS/Supervisor_Drafts/Data, Production, and Aging.docx` needs its pooled-weight examples corrected before it goes further; they are the reason the question landed on the wrong lever. The document is also silent on the retention slope, which is the number a supervisor would actually want.
