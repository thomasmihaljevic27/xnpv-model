- [PROPOSAL] One model family for picks and prospects: the expected value of a team's rights to a
  player, fitted on what those rights actually delivered for comparable assets in past draft classes,
  priced on the contract line, in cap share. The pick curve is its first row (nothing known but the
  slot). Hand-off by rule: the player forecast prices anyone with a 10+ game NHL season in the three
  before the valuation season; the prospect model prices every other rights-holding asset. Outcome
  proposed: the entry-level seasons as delivered, plus the RFA control years at their end valued by
  the player model's own control-year valuation (the contract export starts in 2018, so second
  contracts cannot be measured for the fitting classes). Pick curve: one smooth decreasing curve, shape
  chosen by leaving one class out; one curve for all positions. Prospects: a small regression per
  season since draft on the slot's curve value, age, position and signed status; junior scoring
  (NHLe, 50/30/20 games-weighted) only if it beats that out of sample. Order of work and Karl's likely
  identification questions listed in the chat reply.
- [DECISION] Thomas rejected the standings projection for next-draft picks: a pick slated 55th on
  the trade date is priced as the 55th pick. He had put this to Karl, who agreed a projection would
  be too complicated. Recorded in directive 6. Open detail: the off-season, when no standings exist.
- [DECISION] Round average for picks two or more drafts out: "worth exploring at least". Not decided.
- [DECISION] Entry-level seasons carry the contract-length premium: "They would have to, yes."
- [ASKED] Thomas: how "no future-pick premium" squares with the player model discounting the future.
  Answered from `contract_npv._npv_skater`: the player model discounts each season at 3%
  (`(1 + G) ** -k`) and grows the cap at the same 3% (`cap_path`, `G = CAP_GROWTH`), so a value stated
  as a cap share is unchanged by delay, while fixed-dollar cap hits are discounted. A pick priced the
  same way gets the same 3%; the proposal drops only an extra premium on top of it.
- [ASKED] Thomas: are the RFA years surplus or no surplus? Answered from
  `rfa_terminal_value.terminal_value`: surplus, each control year's market value of his forecast
  minus the qualifying offer, weighted by P(qualified) x P(plays | qualified), stopping at the first
  year the surplus turns negative; the code's own log calls it the team-favourable upper bound.
