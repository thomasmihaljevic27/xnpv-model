- [FINDING, identification] **The level-by-age term is 89% measurement error if fitted the
  obvious way.** The change from t to t+1 is (rate at t+1) minus (rate at t); regressing it on the
  rate at t puts the same noise on both sides, positively in the regressor and negatively in the
  outcome, so the coefficient collects regression to the mean and reports it as aging. Measured on
  the panel: the yearly change correlates **-0.447** with the same-season level and **-0.052** with
  the level one season earlier. Fitted naively the curve claimed a 3-win 27-year-old loses 0.8
  wins a year. **Fix: the lagged level** (season t-1), whose noise is independent of the t-to-t+1
  change by construction. What survives is small and plausible -- gains through about 24, crossing
  over around 25, decline steepening after 30 to -0.38 (0.5-win player) and -0.64 (3-win player) at
  38, so the "more to lose" effect is real and roughly doubles the decline at the top. The naive
  version is registered as a candidate so the register carries the cost of the bias in forecast
  error, not only in a correlation.

- [FINDING, my pre-registered prediction was wrong] I wrote into the code beforehand that the

- [FINDING, identification] **IPW is the wrong tool for this selection.** Reweighting corrects
  selection ON OBSERVABLES -- it assumes that conditional on age, level, games and position,
  being observed is unrelated to the outcome. Here a player retires BECAUSE of the season we do
  not see, so the outcome causes the missingness. It also has a perverse edge: the largest
  weights go to players least likely to survive, and among those, the ones who did survive are
  positively selected, so up-weighting them FLATTENS the curve. IPW stays registered with a
  number against it -- an approach tried and found not to work is evidence; one quietly dropped
  for disagreeing with expectation is not.
- [FINDING, what works] Imputation: a player who played season t and could not hold an NHL job at
  t+1 is assumed to have been at about replacement, zero on this scale. 1,695 missing seasons
  re-enter a panel of 7,764 observed pairs. Stated as an identifying assumption, not a
  measurement, and deliberately a hard case, so the result is a bound on decline rather than a
  point estimate.
- [FINDING, the useful one] **The correction is about level, not age.** It steepens decline for
  good players (3-win at 34: -0.382 to -0.445) and flattens it for marginal ones (0.5-win at 38:
  -0.381 to -0.212). The mechanism is in the data: players who vanish are overwhelmingly marginal
  -- at 33+ their last season averaged +0.060 WAR/82, so imputing them to replacement is a change
  of -0.060 against the -0.30 survivors of the same age show. Fringe players do not fall off a
  cliff, they were already at the floor. **The survivorship bias was never a missing cliff for
  everyone; it was concentrated in the level dimension and was making the model too gentle with
  exactly the players who carry the money.**
