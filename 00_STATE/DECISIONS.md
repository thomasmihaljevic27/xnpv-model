## Resolved decisions (locked — do not reopen without a deliberate revisit)


**Sample windows**
- Back-test window: **intended 2017-18 onward** (Thomas's stated intent; to verify and update). The trades export currently begins effectively 2018-19 (earliest trade 2018-06-23, only one in 2017-18); re-pulling the missing 2017-18 window is a low-priority to-do. The 2005-vs-2013 debate is closed.
- Draft yield curve: **2005-onward** cohorts (separate calibration window; need not match the back-test).

**Data stack**
- Contracts/trades: **PuckPedia** exports are primary; `player_id` is the canonical entity spine (~97% clean join between the two exports).
- Player value: **Patrick Bacon (TopDownHockey / hockeystats.com)** is the single provider for all player-side metrics — skater WAR, goaltender WAR, era-varying NHLe, draft-slot baselines.
- **Single-provider discipline:** do not mix Evolving-Hockey, MoneyPuck, or others into player value. One scoped exception: the self-built NHL API game-level model, used only for mid-season allocation and as the non-Bacon circularity check.
- **Game Value (GV) does NOT replace Bacon for skater valuation (locked 2026-07-04).** A pre-registered four-test battery asked whether the self-built game-level metric could stand in for Bacon skater WAR. Verdict: no — the gap is structural, not a tuning problem. GV-adj (a WAR-like version with RAPM teammate/competition separation, shrunken finishing talent, deployment controls) recovered for forwards (R²=0.504 vs Bacon 0.612) but defencemen stayed essentially flat (R²=0.012 vs Bacon 0.281-0.295), because defensive value is disproportionately about preventing events not visible in shot-and-shift data. **Hybrid roles locked:** Bacon supplies skater value levels; GV-raw proportions Bacon's season value around mid-season trade dates (ratio-based, so teammate confounds partly cancel); GV-adj + GV-raw are the paired Phase 4b circularity validators (GV-adj primary, GV-raw robustness). This tightens single-provider discipline rather than loosening it.
- NTC/NMC clauses: cap-space.com scrape (~1,672 players, 15,261 contract-season rows, 97.7% hit). Validated internally; cross-checked vs CapWages (93.4% agreement). Join key `puckpedia_ep_id`; dedupe on `player_id + signing_date + cap_hit` before join. The 5-7% source disagreement is treated as measurement error, not a disqualifier.
- `trades.db` (CapFriendly, 2004-2022): contingency only, off critical path; needs 2018-2022 spot-validation before any use.
- **Dead plan:** the old MoneyPuck / Natural Stat Trick / Hockey-Reference / Selenium-PuckPedia approach is retired. Do not reference as current.

**NHLe methodology**
- Bacon era-varying coefficients (`nhle_temporal.csv`) are chosen — justified by survivor-bias correction and era-varying breaks (to be documented in the model overview). Supersedes all static NHLe tables; never mix.

**Modeling conventions**
- Outputs are **surplus ratios**, not raw dollar differentials.
- Player value attaches from **trailing seasons (t-1, t-2)**, never the trade-season row.
- Mid-season trades charged prorated cap cost; mid-season value allocation pending (Work Queue P4).
- Dollars-per-win rate is **market-derived** (GM opportunity cost) -> market-relative value. **UPDATED 2026-07-28:** no longer position-blind — position enters as a slope interaction (see Regression Results, Stage 3 rate). Replacement cost = the regression intercept, not the league minimum (intercept is now shared across positions; only the per-win slope differs). **Two regressions only — RFA and UFA;** ELC value runs through the RFA regression (projected production priced at the RFA rate, minus slotted ELC cost), not a separate ELC fit.
- RFA terminal value uses the **WAR-only coefficient**, not full controls.
- Elite Prospects `rights`/`status` are as-of-scrape-date; never use them to classify historical roster status.
- Bacon's model embeds author-confirmed look-ahead (post-hoc career outcomes): documented in limitations, not fatal.
- Same-name collisions needing manual disambiguation: **Sebastian Aho, Elias Pettersson, Connor Murphy, Josh Anderson**.

**Phase 1b decisions (locked 2026-07-05 — D1-D10)**
- **D1 — market label:** each contract-*season* is labeled RFA/UFA by the player's control status that season (`season_start < ufa_year` -> RFA), not by the market the contract was originally signed in. Handles contracts that straddle RFA-into-UFA years. Metadata only under D7 (does not change the price).
- **D2/D6 — the WAR_AAV_Regression_Report_v2 rate was goalie-contaminated.** Reproducing the locked report byte-for-byte (guard in `skater_value_engine.py`, Stage 0a) revealed its sample pooled goalies in with skaters — never documented in the report's methods section. Goalies are 12.5% of the old UFA sample and 7.4% of RFA, on a different WAR scale, and this diluted the skater dollars-per-WAR rate by ~26% while goalies separately got their own engine on 2026-06-30 — a mis-specification (pooling populations whose x-variable means different things) that, once found, could not be kept. **Skater-only re-derivation adopted (identical spec otherwise).**
- **D7 — ONE skater market, not two.** The RFA/UFA "distinctness" in the old pooled report (F=3.63, p=0.0266) does not survive goalie removal: skater-only F=0.12, p=0.887 — statistically indistinguishable. The asymmetric goalie share across the two old samples was manufacturing the apparent gap. A single rate now prices both markets; ELC-via-RFA and RFA terminal value are unaffected (there is simply one rate to apply instead of two).
- **D8 — regression estimation window moved to 2018-2025 starts** (from 2015-2025). The PuckPedia export's coverage of 2015-2017 is thin and selected (63/223/452 contracts for 2015/16/17 vs a stable ~680-790/yr from 2018 on) — surviving early contracts are a non-representative slice (fitted intercepts ~4.8% cap vs a stable ~1.8-2.0% every year from 2018). This also aligns the rate's estimation window with the back-test window. **Supersedes the flat-cap Chow-break finding below** (see Regression Results).
- **D9 — no era/regime split in the skater rate.** Within the 2018+ window, the "flat-cap regime" Chow break is no longer clearly present (2018-19 vs flat-cap p=0.75); the only surviving structure is a marginal post-2023 cheapening (p=0.044), treated as noise for now. One flat rate is used; era-specific rates remain available as a robustness re-run. This makes D2b (which regime-keying convention to use for Layer 2 trade-date projections) moot until that robustness pass is run.
- **D10 — Value_t floors at the league-minimum base salary** for that season (2015-16: $575K ... 2025-26: $775K, per-season table in `skater_value_engine.py`). Rationale: a replacement-level player can always be signed at the league minimum, so no player's modeled production value should price below it. The floor is an exogenous, CBA-set constant — it uses no outcome data, so it introduces no circularity, look-ahead, or selection-bias exposure. Binds on 5.3% of priced rows (368/6,892), adding $182M in aggregate Value_t across the sample — a small, sane correction concentrated on deep-negative-trailing-WAR seasons.

**Phase 1b Layer 2 + Phase 1c/1d/1a decisions (locked 2026-07-05 — D11-D19)**
- **D3 (locked earlier, applied here) — aging curve as a decay PATH, not a level substitute.** Layer 2 projects future contract seasons by anchoring on the trailing 60/40 raw WAR (the exact Layer 1 number) and multiplying by the *ratio* path the locked aging curve implies (each future per-82 level ÷ its own starting level). Raw units in, raw units out — no unit mismatch with the locked $/WAR rate. Positive-anchor backcast (2018-2022, ~2,200 players/horizon) beats hold-flat by +8.7/+6.7/+8.3% at k=1/2/3 (MAE ≈ 0.47/0.70/0.78 WAR, ~$0.95-1.57M).
- **D11 — ex-ante cap path.** Future cap ceilings in any projection = the valuation season's known ceiling grown at 3%/yr, NEVER the realized future ceiling (a 2019 valuation must not "know" COVID froze the cap). Extends the trade-date-information-set principle. Both sides of a trade share the path, so path error largely cancels in surplus ratios. League minimums use the CBA's published forward schedule (genuinely ex-ante over most horizons; 2026+ steps come from the 2025 MOU — third-order vintage caveat, documented).
- **D12 v3 — negative-anchor handling (replacement reversion).** Full trail preserved for the paper appendix: v1 froze any low anchor flat (rejected — threw away curve info); v2 was Thomas's reflection rule, reflecting the multiplier around 1 for negative anchors (theoretically clean, beat hold-flat at k=1 +1.8% but INVERTED at longer horizons −4.1%/−8.3%); v3 (locked) keeps the real anchor at k=0 and projects every future season at 0 WAR (replacement). Diagnosis that killed v2: below-replacement players who keep playing overwhelmingly bounce back (93% improved at k=1, 81% ended positive, mean −0.22→+0.32) — a negative trailing WAR is mostly noise, and the aging curve is the wrong instrument for it; mean-reversion-to-replacement is right. v3 beat every alternative at every horizon (MAE 0.364/0.569/0.562 vs hold-flat 0.551/0.733/0.662; +34.0/+22.3/+15.1%). Applies to ALL negative anchors, curve-eligible or not.
- **D13 — RFA terminal-value truncation.** Control-year rights are a right, not an obligation: the terminal value truncates at the first control year whose projected surplus goes negative (the team walks away, and the rights vanish for all later years — you cannot skip a year and re-qualify). Raw untruncated sum also reported. Binds on only ~0.1% of contracts under the current value frame (see D14).
- **D14(c) — fringe terminal-value calibration.** The locked "replacement cost = intercept" convention makes a 0-WAR control year worth ~1.85% of cap while its QO costs less, so nearly every control year reads as worth qualifying — the model predicted only 18% of the 614 real walk-aways in-window. A net-of-replacement frame over-corrected (wrongly zeroed 44% of players teams kept). Resolution (locked): weight each control year by the OBSERVED qualify rate for players of that projected quality, calibrated from real qualify-or-walk decisions in the spine, gates compounding per year. Re-estimated from the spine every run, no hardcoded rates. **Rates as of the 2026-07-28 local run (post-Stage-4 fix): star 100.0% (n=22), regular 93.2% (n=190), fringe 79.3% (n=595), below-replacement 67.3% (n=1,463), total n=2,270.** The pre-fix table (star 100.0%/22, regular 93.2%/190, fringe 79.3%/595, negative 70.3%/424, total n=1,231) is reported alongside on every run so the correction stays visible. Effect on terminal values at the 2026-07-28 rate: fringe median $1.70M→$1.12M, below-replacement $0.67M→$0.46M.
- **QO era rule (locked as built).** Qualifying offers computed on base salary, one year, iterated year-over-year: old CBA bands (≤$660K→110%; <$1M→105% cap $1M; ≥$1M→100%, +120%-of-cap-hit rule for 2020+-signed as a proxy on start year) through summer 2026; new 2026 CBA bands (≤$1.25M→110%; <$1.75M→105% cap $1.75M; ≥$1.75M→min(100%, 120% cap hit)) from offseason 2026 on. Every QO floored at that season's league minimum. The 2026 bands were unknowable pre-Sept-2025 — third-order ex-ante caveat, documented not modeled. Mechanics assert-self-test on import.
- **D15 — GM impatience excluded from r.** The discount rate is normative/fundamentals-only. If r were calibrated to how GMs actually behave, the back-test could not detect the future-asset mispricing it exists to find (it would assume its own conclusion). GM over-discounting is a FINDING the back-test measures, never an input. (Standard sports-econ identification, cf. the NFL draft-pick over-discounting literature.)
- **D16(i) — discount structure.** Survival probability multiplies the VALUE side (a player who exits stops producing) while cap growth sits in the denominator — chosen over a flat r = g + h because exits kill production but not necessarily the cap hit (injured/buried players still count). Retirement voiding a cap hit is a documented simplification; keeping cost unconditional errs conservative on NPV.
- **D17 — g = 3%, reused from D11.** One cap-growth assumption, two uses (growing ex-ante ceilings AND discounting). They mesh: value holds constant in cap-share terms while a fixed cap hit gets relatively cheaper each year ("good contracts age well in a rising cap"), and cross-year dollars become comparable in today's cap-share units.
- **D18 — exit hazard (h) estimated from the panel.** For every GP≥10 skater-season (the priced population), the fraction with NO NHL season at t+1, by quality×age bucket, transition years 2018-2024 (matches the back-test window AND the range where the age join is complete — older years would undercount exits, since the age join misses precisely the pre-2018 retirees). Findings: established players barely exit (star 1.1%, regular 0.9%/yr) — exits are a fringe/negative phenomenon (10.3%/19.4%) that compounds hard with age (35+ fringe 33%, 35+ negative 52%). Robustness: year-by-year stable, COVID transition was LOWER not higher, 18.4% of "exiters" return within two years (so raw hazard mildly overstates permanent loss — conservative direction). Care-point built in: h captures ONLY total-exit events — the aging curve (within-player deltas, survivors only) and D12 v3 (collapse-while-playing) handle decline, so no double-count. Control years carry the D14(c) qualify gates INSTEAD of h (those gates already internalize departure at the tender decision — applying both would double-count).
- **D19 — goalie forward projection (flat) + λ CONVENTION CORRECTION.** No goalie aging curve exists (locked earlier); goalie projection = trailing 50/30/20 cascade → shrink toward the league-average goalie → held flat over the horizon (the shrinkage IS the mean-reversion device). **Correction discovered building 1d:** the documented goalie shrinkage "λ=0.65, surprisingly close to the skater 0.55" has the label flipped. λ=0.65 is the weight on the LEAGUE AVERAGE; goalies KEEP 35% of trailing (vs skaters keeping 55%) — much harder shrinkage, as a-priori expected from goalies' weaker year-to-year signal. Confirmed three ways: (a) `goalie_value_spine.csv`'s stored `shrunk_projection` backs out kept=0.35 exactly; (b) head-to-head on 645 goalie-seasons, kept-0.35 predicts next-season WAR better (MAE 2.084 vs 2.131); (c) reliability logic (optimal kept ≈ persistence r ≈ 0.26-0.35). The shipped spine was RIGHT; the narrative wording is wrong — corrected in Regression Results / Player Model Progress Log. Goalie rookies (no NHL history) now return unpriced → prospect pillar, mirroring the skater no-anchor rule (the old league-average fallback wrongly priced unknown ELC goalies as average NHL starters — it had put them atop the entire NPV list). Goalie exit hazard estimated separately from `Goalies_WAR.csv` (overall 11.55%/yr, same age gradient).

**Phase 3a decisions (locked 2026-07-14/2026-07-19 — D22-D27): the draft-pick yield curve**

- **Aging-curve split-sample stability check — RUN AND PASSED.** Karl's defence for fitting the curve on the whole panel is that how a player ages is a fact about human beings rather than about any particular era, so applying it at every decision date is not look-ahead. That was asserted. It is now tested. Method: **within-player age deltas** (the change in per-82 WAR from age a to a+1 for the same man) rather than age-group means, because comparing the average 24-year-old to the average 32-year-old confuses aging with survivorship. Early era 2007-2015 against late 2016-2025, differences tested by a **player-clustered bootstrap** (resampling players, not player-seasons, since one player contributes many correlated rows). Result on 7,530 usable deltas across 1,479 players: **forwards differ at 0 of 16 ages; defence at 1 of 16** (age 30, where the early cell has n=30). Across 32 tests at 95% chance alone predicts about 1.6 false positives, so one flag is what no real difference looks like. Thomas's local run reproduced every figure digit for digit. - **Control-year departure is charged inconsistently across positions — TESTED, both fixes PARKED.** The model asks whether a club will TENDER a restricted free agent. It never asks whether he then actually played. The test that separates those two events took every qualify-or-walk decision and asked whether the player appeared in the league the following season, **conditioned on his being an NHL regular at the decision** — without that conditioning roughly half of every apparent disappearance is an AHL player who was never in the league to leave it, and the exit rates come out absurd. Result: of players a club walked away from, **41.9% of skaters and 42.9% of goaltenders also left the league**, so the tender gate measures club retention rather than league exit and the two only partly overlap. Skaters and goaltenders are statistically indistinguishable on this, which removes any basis for treating the positions differently. Two consequences follow, neither actioned:
  - **Skaters (a D14(c) reopen).** Conditional on being tendered, a skater still exits at **13.1%/yr**, none of it counted. The correct control-year weight is P(tendered) x P(plays | tendered) and only the first term is in. Not double-charging: the 42% overlap sits in the *walked* branch, which the gate already zeroes, while this sits in the disjoint *kept* branch. Corrected weights at a one-game bar: below-replacement 0.694→0.619, fringe 0.795→0.753, regular 0.888→0.877, star unchanged. Compounded over three control years for a below-replacement player: 0.334 against 0.237, a factor of 1.41.
  - **Goaltenders (a new decision, and the larger of the two).** Goalie control years are weighted by **nothing at all** — the goalie branch of `contract_npv.py` writes `survival: 1.0` on every terminal row and its own comment records the missing gate as a known gap. A goaltender with three control years is valued as though he is certain to still be there. Pooled at a one-game bar: P(tendered) 0.909, corrected 0.788, n=99. Compounded that is 0.788 / 0.621 / 0.489 across three years against 1.0 / 1.0 / 1.0 today.
  - **Two sub-decisions already made by Thomas:** a single **pooled** goalie rate rather than four talent buckets (the goalie cells cannot support buckets — fringe n=5, regular n=8 — while pooled n=99 supports one number); and a **one-game bar** for materialisation rather than the 20-game bar the exit hazard uses, because the gate asks whether the control year happened at all and a surviving player who plays five games is overvalued through the projection instead. The bar matters enormously: at 20 games the bottom-bucket correction reads 29.5% instead of 12.2%, so **more than half of the naive correction is a threshold artifact**.
- **Future-pick discount premium — INVESTIGATED AND SHELVED with the draft model.** The pure pick-for-pick sample cannot identify it, and the reason is itself a finding. Sample: 119 pure pick trades, 56 cross-year, of which 39 are one-for-one and 35 of those are same-round swaps carrying almost no information, leaving 17 bundle trades. A single discount factor fitted to the bundles gives 0.486 (round-mean slot convention) or 0.510 (team-own-slot), but the **per-trade implied values run 0.015 to 0.689** and are ordered almost perfectly by how large the future sweetener is relative to the slot gap it buys. **The driver is a minimum-denomination floor:** clubs cannot trade cash, so the smallest unit of consideration is a late-round pick, which the curve prices near $0.81M. Any trade closing a gap smaller than that is a **corner solution rather than an equilibrium price**, and equality-based estimation applied to a corner is misspecified. Sweetener size does scale with the gap (Spearman 0.735), so clubs are pricing directionally and are floored, not confused. **Carried forward:** estimate the premium as **bounds** rather than a point, since each corner trade yields an inequality restriction; and a **repeat-sales design** over the 163 picks traded at two or more different horizons is the stronger identification, but it needs the other side of each trade priced and so belongs in Phase 4 (only 4 of the 163 have all legs inside pure-pick trades; 84 have at least one). Also found: **PuckPedia's `overall_position` is back-filled for future picks** — a 2020 third traded in June 2019 carries its realized slot 70, a number nobody knew on the day. It must never be read for an ex-ante price.
